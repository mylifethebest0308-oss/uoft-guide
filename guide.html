Applying to U of T

How to Apply

Almost everyone applies through OUAC (one website that handles applications for every Ontario university). One application covers up to 3 U of T programs.

Where to apply

When
Late SeptemberOUAC application opens
November 7Recommended early application date — apply by this + documents by Dec 2 to be in the first admission round
January 15Main deadline for most programs (some close earlier — always check your program)
How much
OUAC base fee$159 — covers your first 3 program choices

Sending Grades

How your grades reach U of T depends on where you go to school. Never email anything — it all goes through the Join U of T portal (the website U of T gives every applicant).

By where you study
Ontario high school (currently enrolled)Do nothing — your school sends Grade 11–12 results automatically through OUAC. Unsolicited transcripts aren't reviewed.
Quebec CEGEPEnter your Permanent Code on the OUAC application; U of T gets your transcripts electronically.
Other Canadian provinces / territoriesAfter applying, upload your transcripts AND complete the self-reported grades form on Join U of T.
U.S. high schoolSend transcripts electronically via Parchment/Naviance; SAT/ACT/AP scores must be sent electronically too.
IB diplomaUpload transcripts + self-report; final IB results must come electronically from the IBO.
GCE / British-patternedUpload transcripts; final IGCSE/GCSE/AS/A-Level results must be issued by the exam board.
Other international high schoolsUpload transcripts (+ self-report if requested); check Join U of T for your exact document list.

Money & Dates

Applying costs money up front, and enrolling costs a lot more later. Here's every payment in order, plus the two things you must do on time: claim your aid, and accept your offer.

Payment dates
When you apply (Sept–Jan)OUAC fees — $159 base for 3 choices, $51 per extra choice, plus U of T's $96 document evaluation if you're not a current Ontario high school student. Non-refundable, and your application isn't forwarded until paid.
Feb–May (with your offer)Admission deposit (money you pay to hold your spot) — some offers require one through ACORN (U of T's student website). Your offer letter says how much and by when.
March 31Residence application — apply by this date for the first-year residence guarantee (a residence deposit follows once you accept a room offer).
August 11Minimum payment to register (the first chunk of tuition) — pay it, or your courses get dropped. The single most important payment date of the year.
September 30Fall term balance — the rest of your Fall tuition. Pay late and interest (extra charges) starts October 15.
November 30Winter term balance — the rest of your Winter tuition. Interest starts December 15.
Scholarships
October–NovemberA few scholarships require their own application and close this early — check the official awards page as soon as you apply.
February 2Awards Profile (a short form about your money situation, leadership and activities) — fill it in on the Join U of T portal. Skip it and you're simply not considered for those awards.
Financial aid
February 2Deadline for UTAPS (U of T's own money help for students who need it) — it covers what government aid doesn't.
Ontario residentsApply for OSAP (Ontario's government student aid — part free money, part loan).
Other provincesApply through your home province's student aid program — then UTAPS can top up unmet need.
Accepting your offer
Where you acceptOn the OUAC website, not the U of T portal.
June 2Acceptance deadline for current Ontario high school students. If yours differs, it's written in your offer letter — late responses are only accepted at the university's discretion.
Most offers are conditionalMost offers are conditional (they depend on your final grades). Keep your marks up — U of T checks them, and can take the offer back.
September 11Deadline to ask for a deferral (starting one year later instead). The form opens on your offer page in July — approval isn't guaranteed.

UofT Glossary (35 terms)

TermPlain-language meaning
Course codeEvery course's unique ID, like PSY100H1. The letters = subject, the number = level, and the letter near the end tells you the credit weight (H or Y).
CreditThe weight of a course. Almost every course is 0.5 credit (an “H” in the code) or 1.0 credit (a “Y”). You need 20.0 credits in total to graduate.
Course weightSame idea as credit — how much a course counts, based on its teaching hours. With few exceptions it's 0.5 or 1.0.
FCE (Full-Course Equivalent)Older way of saying 1.0 credit. If someone says “1 FCE”, they mean one full credit; “0.5 FCE” is a half course.
Lecture (L)The standard class format — the instructor teaches, you listen and take notes. In course listings, “36L” means 36 hours of lecture.
Tutorial (T)A smaller group session for discussing lecture material and asking questions, usually run by a TA (teaching assistant). “24T” = 24 tutorial hours.
Practical (P)Hands-on sessions — for science courses this means labs. “18P” = 18 hours of practical work.
Seminar (S)A small discussion-heavy class, common in upper years — part lecture, part structured discussion, often with student presentations. “24S” = 24 seminar hours.
PrerequisiteA course you must finish before taking another one — sometimes with a minimum grade (e.g. “CSC148 with at least 60%”).
Co-requisiteA course you must take at the same time as (or before) another course.
ExclusionA course you can't get credit for because it overlaps too much with one you've already taken (or are taking now). Check this before enrolling — taking an exclusion wastes money and credit.
Recommended preparationA course the department suggests taking first, but it's not required. You can enrol without it.
Extra (EXT)A course marked EXT on your transcript doesn't count toward your 20.0 credits. This happens to courses you designate as Extra.
Letter of PermissionOfficial pre-approval to take a course at another university and transfer the credit back to U of T. Get this approved before you enrol elsewhere.
SpecialistThe deepest program type — 10.0 to 14.0 credits in one subject. Usually the main focus of your whole degree. Program codes start with ASSPE.
MajorA solid, medium-depth program — 6.0 to 8.0 credits. Usually paired with another Major or two Minors. Codes start with ASMAJ.
MinorThe lightest program — 4.0 credits. An add-on next to a Major or Specialist, never on its own. Codes start with ASMIN.
FocusA small optional cluster of courses inside a Specialist or Major that shows you concentrated on a theme (e.g. Data Analytics within the Economics Major). Codes start with ASFOC.
StreamA flavour of a program listed as its own entry — e.g. Cognitive Science Major has separate Language, Perception, and Thinking streams, each with its own code.
Program codeEvery program's ID: ASSPE… = Specialist, ASMAJ… = Major, ASMIN… = Minor, ASFOC… = Focus. You can search these codes on ACORN and in the Calendar.
Open enrolment programA program you can join with no conditions other than finishing 4.0 credits. Just request it on ACORN and you're in.
Limited enrolment programA program with extra requirements beyond 4.0 credits — minimum grades in certain courses, sometimes an application, and sometimes limited seats.
Subject POSt (Program of Study)The official name for your program on ACORN. “Requesting a POSt” = applying to enrol in a Specialist/Major/Minor, which you do near the end of first year.
CertificateA short for-credit credential. Category 2 certificates can be done alongside your undergrad degree; Category 1 usually requires a finished degree first.
GPA (Grade Point Average)Your average grade, weighted by course size (a 1.0-credit course counts double a 0.5). U of T uses a 4.0 scale.
CR/NCR (Credit / No Credit)An option that puts CR (credit) or NCR (no credit) on your transcript instead of a percentage mark — the course still counts toward your 20.0 credits if you pass, but doesn't affect GPA. Some courses aren't eligible.
LWD (Late Withdrawal after the Drop Date)Lets you leave a course after the drop deadline (up to the last day of classes) without a mark — the transcript shows LWD instead. You can use this for at most 3.0 credits total, no petition needed.
Academic standingYour academic status based on GPA. The four levels: In Good Standing, On Probation, On Suspension, and Refused Further Registration.
PetitionA formal request asking the Faculty to make an exception to a rule for you — e.g. a deadline extension for documented circumstances.
Language citationA transcript recognition for advanced language study. It's not a program and doesn't count toward your program requirements — it's a bonus line on your record.
Year of studyDecided by credits finished, not calendar years: under 4.0 credits = Year 1, 4.0–8.5 = Year 2, 9.0–13.5 = Year 3, 14.0+ = Year 4.
ACORNU of T's student portal (acorn.utoronto.ca) — where you enrol in courses and programs, check your invoice, and see your grades.
QuercusThe online course hub (q.utoronto.ca) — where instructors post the syllabus, readings, assignments, announcements, and marks during the term.
Degree ExplorerThe official degree-planning tool — shows which degree and program requirements you've completed and what's still missing.
Timetable BuilderThe official timetable site (ttb.utoronto.ca) — look up when and where every course section runs before you enrol on ACORN.

  • Society and Its Institutions (3) — shown on this site as “Social & Behavioural Sciences”

  • The Physical and Mathematical Universes (5) — shown on this site as “Quantitative Reasoning”

To graduate: at least 1.0 credit in each of 4 of the 5 categories, or 1.0 in each of any 3 plus 0.5 in each of the other 2 (official rule). The main site lists 123 breadth courses with student reviews.
